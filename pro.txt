The homepage presents Zyverra Labs as a global AI and software studio and never mentions aesthetics clinics, the UK, £ prices, the admin dashboard or your three clinic websites. This report covers the audit, the keyword and page plan, and ends with one copy-paste prompt for your coding agent.

## TL;DR

- **The biggest problem is how the site is positioned, not technical SEO.** The homepage title is "Zyverra Labs | AI Agents, Automation & Custom Software", the H1 is "We build AI Agents for modern teams.", and the portfolio shows LandChain, VAREZA and PDF Merger, not clinic websites. So Google, ChatGPT and Perplexity have nothing that links "Zyverra Labs" to "UK aesthetics clinic websites". The fix: one page per service that search engines can read, £ pricing, the clinic portfolio, UK English and the same business details everywhere.
- **Compete where other agencies don't.** "Aesthetic clinic website design" is crowded with UK specialists (Aesthetic Web, Ashrose, BoutiqueSEO, AETHUS, Podium Design, Aesthetics Desk), and Aesthetics Desk sells sites from £350, live in 5 days, which is cheaper than your £400 offer. Almost no specialist targets searches about a self-edit admin dashboard, clear £300/£400 pricing or AI search visibility. Lead with those.
- **Do the AI-search (AEO) work in this order.** (1) Pages that search engines can read, which answer the question in the first lines, with accurate schema markup. (2) Bing Webmaster Tools: in a 2025 Seer Interactive study, 87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top results, compared with 56% for Google's; a later 400-query test found only 27%, so treat this as a direction, not a fixed number. (3) Mentions of your brand on other websites: Ahrefs' 75,000-brand study found mentions correlated 0.664 with appearing in Google AI Overviews, against 0.218 for backlinks (sites rated DR 40+; correlation, not proof of cause). An llms.txt file is optional and cheap, but no reliable evidence shows it helps. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026, according to its Search Central changelog, but FAQPage schema still has value.

Slug: `botox-advertising-rules-clinic-website` · Title tag: "Can You Say 'Botox' on Your Website? UK ASA Rules" · Meta: "Botox is a prescription-only medicine, so UK clinics can't advertise it to the public. Here's what the ASA allows on your website and social media."

# Can you say "Botox" on your clinic website? UK ASA rules explained

**Short answer:** In most cases, no. Botox and other botulinum toxin brands, such as Vistabel, Dysport, Azzalure and Bocouture, are prescription-only medicines (POMs). In the UK, the ASA's guidance is blunt: "prescription-only medicines… simply may not be advertised to the public". Indirect references can count as advertising too. Your clinic website, Instagram, Facebook and paid ads all count as advertising. This guide covers what you can say instead.

*This is general guidance, not legal advice. Always check the current guidance from the ASA and CAP.*

## Why the rule exists

The Committee of Advertising Practice (CAP) writes the UK advertising rules, and the Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) enforces them. CAP Code rule 12.12 states that prescription-only medicines "may not be advertised to the public". This sits alongside the Human Medicines Regulations 2012, which ban any advertisement "likely to lead to the use of a prescription only medicine". The idea is that a prescriber, not an advert, should decide whether you need a medicine.

The ASA has published an Enforcement Notice specifically about advertising Botox and other botulinum toxin injections on social media, and it actively monitors clinics.

## What counts as "advertising" a POM

It's broader than most practitioners expect. The ASA's FAQs and Enforcement Notice cover:

- **The brand name itself**: "Botox", "Vistabel", "Azzalure" and others.
- **Implied references.** The ASA's Botox FAQs state that "the word 'relaxing' is likely to be understood as an implied reference to Botox", so "wrinkle-relaxing injections" is a problem. Nicknames and plays on words are treated the same way.
- **Prices.** The Enforcement Notice warns that "anti-wrinkle injections" shown alongside a price that relates to a POM will be seen as an ad for that POM.
- **Juxtaposition.** If you write "anti-wrinkle injections and dermal fillers", the "injections" will be read as Botox.
- **Images** that clearly show a POM treatment being promoted.

## What you *can* say

The ASA's own FAQs give clinics some room:

- **Use a collective term for your anti-wrinkle options.** The ASA suggests "anti-wrinkle injections" or "anti-wrinkle treatments" as a collective term, *if* you offer both prescription-only and non-prescription-only options. If you only offer the POM, it advises against "anti-wrinkle injections" because the phrase becomes an implied ad.
- **Promote consultations, not the medicine.** You can promote a consultation for a concern, such as lines and wrinkles, as long as it's clear the consultation may or may not lead to a prescription.
- **Name non-POM treatments.** Many dermal fillers, skin peels, microneedling and laser treatments are not POMs and can be described normally. Some fillers containing lidocaine may be classed differently, so check each product.
- **Answer questions factually where the rules allow.** In its "dos and don'ts of advertising Botox" guidance, the ASA says limited references may be acceptable "as long as they do not appear on the home page" and are "balanced and factual". It adds that "casually browsing consumers must not be able to come across information relating to Botox or other POMs with ease". Don't assume your page qualifies.

## How this affects your website structure

Practical changes we make on every aesthetics site:

1. **Treatment pages are built around concerns, not brand names.** For example: "Lines and wrinkles consultation", "Lip enhancement with dermal filler", "Skin rejuvenation".
2. **Price lists separate POM and non-POM treatments.** POM prices are not listed in a promotional way. We use "Price on consultation" or follow current ASA wording guidance.
3. **No "Brotox", "Baby Tox", "tox" or "wrinkle-relaxing"**, anywhere, including image alt text, file names and meta descriptions. Search engines index all of these.
4. **Offers.** Avoid time-limited or "buy now" promotions for injectable treatments. CAP's guidance on cosmetic interventions also warns against trivialising procedures and pressuring people into quick decisions.
5. **No targeting of under-18s** for cosmetic procedures.
6. **Before-and-after images** must be genuine, unedited and representative. If you offer both POM and non-POM treatments, make clear which treatment a photo shows.

## "But my competitors say Botox everywhere"

Some do, and some are complained about and named in ASA rulings. Ranking for "Botox [town]" isn't worth an ASA ruling, a forced rewrite of your site or questions from your insurer. Treatment-concern pages ("lines and wrinkles", "frown lines", "excessive sweating consultation") can still attract people searching for help. Clear, factual, helpful content is also exactly what Google and AI assistants favour.

## A quick compliance checklist for your site

- [ ] Search your site for "Botox" and other POM brand names, and remove promotional uses.
- [ ] Search for "relaxing", "tox" and nicknames.
- [ ] Check price lists for POM prices displayed promotionally.
- [ ] Check offers and countdown banners on injectables.
- [ ] Check image alt text, file names, page titles and meta descriptions.
- [ ] Check your Google Business Profile services and social bios.
- [ ] Make sure before-and-after images are consented and labelled.

# How to get your aesthetics clinic recommended by ChatGPT and Google AI

**Short answer:** AI assistants recommend clinics they can find, understand and trust. In practice that means five things: a fast website that search engines can crawl, with clear, answer-first treatment pages; accurate structured data; being indexed in both Google *and* Bing (ChatGPT's live search leans on Bing); consistent business details everywhere; and your clinic's name being mentioned on other reputable websites. No one can guarantee a recommendation, but these are the foundations every recommendation is built on.

## How AI assistants actually find clinics

When someone asks "Who's a good aesthetics clinic in Leeds for skin treatments?", the assistant usually runs a live web search and then summarises what it finds. The sources differ by platform:

- **Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** use Google's normal Search index. Google's guide to optimising for its generative AI features says: "For Google Search, you can ignore tactics like 'chunking' content, creating unnecessary AI text files (like llms.txt), or pursuing inauthentic mentions."
- **ChatGPT search** relies heavily on Bing's index and third-party data. A February 2025 Seer Interactive study found that 87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top results, compared with 56% for Google's. A larger replication found a much lower overlap, so treat this as directional. Either way, a site that Bing hasn't indexed is effectively invisible to it.
- **Perplexity** uses its own crawler and index.
- **Gemini** draws on Google's systems.

So "AI SEO" is mostly good SEO, done for more than one search engine.

## Step 1: Make your website easy to read

- **One page per treatment or concern.** Open each with a two- or three-sentence direct answer: what it is, who it's for, the price or range, and how to book.
- **Plain HTML text.** Avoid putting key information only inside images, sliders or PDFs.
- **Fast and mobile-friendly.** Google's guidance lists page experience among the basics.
- **An FAQ section** answering real client questions in full sentences.

## Step 2: Add accurate structured data

Schema markup tells machines exactly who you are: your business type, address, opening hours, services, prices and practitioners. Use LocalBusiness (or a more specific type), Service, FAQPage and BreadcrumbList. Google says no *special* schema is required for AI features, but correct schema removes ambiguity about your clinic's name, location and services. Only mark up information that is visible on the page.

## Step 3: Get indexed in Bing as well as Google

1. Verify your site in **Google Search Console** and submit your sitemap. Google now offers a generative AI performance report, which helps you see how your content performs in its AI features.
2. Verify your site in **Bing Webmaster Tools**. You can import it from Search Console in minutes. Submit the sitemap and enable IndexNow so new pages are picked up quickly.
3. Claim **Bing Places for Business** as well as your **Google Business Profile**, and keep the two identical.

## Step 4: Keep your details consistent everywhere

Your clinic's name, address, phone number, opening hours and treatments should match on your website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, social profiles, booking platforms and directories. Conflicting details make it harder for AI systems to be confident about your clinic.

## Step 5: Earn mentions on other websites

This is the step most clinics skip, and it's probably the most important.

- In a 2025 study of 75,000 brands with a Domain Rating above 40, Ahrefs found that **branded web mentions** had the strongest Spearman correlation with visibility in Google AI Overviews: 0.664, compared with 0.218 for backlinks.
- Ahrefs' December 2025 follow-up across ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews found **YouTube mentions** correlated even more strongly, at roughly 0.737, the strongest signal it measured.

Ahrefs itself stresses these are correlations, not proof of cause, but the direction is clear. Ways to earn mentions: reviews on Google and other platforms; features in local press or trade publications; supplier and training-academy "find a practitioner" listings; talks, podcasts and YouTube videos; genuine community discussion.

Avoid paid fake reviews or spammy listicles. Google explicitly says inauthentic mentions are not the way.

## Step 6: Keep content fresh

AI assistants and search engines favour clinics that look active: current prices, new treatments, recent blog posts and up-to-date results. This is where an **admin dashboard** helps. If you can update your own site in minutes, it stays accurate without waiting for a developer.

## What about llms.txt?

llms.txt is a proposed text file that summarises your site for AI tools. It's cheap to add, but don't expect miracles. Google says it is not needed for Google Search. Ahrefs' June 2026 study checked 137,210 domains and found about 38,000 with a valid llms.txt. Of those, 97% received zero requests in May 2026, and AI retrieval bots made only 1.1% of the requests that did occur. Add one if you like, but put your effort into steps 1–6.

## A 30-minute starter checklist

- [ ] Search ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity for "[your treatment] clinic in [your town]", and note who appears.
- [ ] Check your site is indexed in Bing (search `site:yourdomain.co.uk` on Bing).
- [ ] Make sure your Google Business Profile and Bing Places details match your website exactly.
- [ ] Add a direct-answer opening paragraph to your top three treatment pages.
- [ ] Ask five happy clients for a review this week.

## G7. Performance and accessibility

*Core Web Vitals targets (mobile, field data):* LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1.

*Images:* use `next/image` everywhere with correct `sizes`, so images stop requesting `w=3840`; convert the `.PNG` portfolio screenshots to AVIF/WebP at sensible widths; use `priority` only on the hero LCP image and lazy-load everything else; give every image explicit width and height; write descriptive alt text in UK English and use `alt=""` for decorative images; de-duplicate the industry marquee images, or mark the duplicates `aria-hidden`.

*Fonts and scripts:* use `next/font` with `display: swap`; remove unused JS; load the chatbot widget and any third-party scripts after interaction or idle (`next/script` `strategy="lazyOnload"`).

*Accessibility:* visible focus states and contrast ≥4.5:1; labelled form fields; keyboard-operable dropdown nav and accordions; keep the existing skip link and make sure it targets `<main id="main">`; `prefers-reduced-motion` disables marquee and counter animations.

## G8. Analytics, Search Console, Bing, IndexNow

*Analytics:* use first-party, privacy-friendly analytics for aggregate statistics only (for example Vercel Web Analytics or Plausible), so it can rely on the UK statistical-purposes exemption with a clear opt-out (see Step 8). GA4 and the Meta Pixel load **only after consent** in the marketing category. Fire these events through one consent-aware `track(eventName, params)` helper: `whatsapp_click`, `quote_form_submit`, `book_call_click`, `email_click`, `pricing_view_option_a/b`.

*Verification and indexing:* add Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verification via env vars (`NEXT_PUBLIC_GSC_VERIFICATION`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_BING_VERIFICATION`) as `<meta>` tags, left empty if not provided. IndexNow: generate a key; serve `/{key}.txt`; add a small script `scripts/indexnow.ts` that POSTs changed URLs to `https://api.indexnow.org/indexnow` after deploy or post publish; document how to run it.
